Which linearised tangent directions are actually tangent to the variety?
The idempotency and tightness constraints linearise to a space of dimension
sum over vertex pairs of (|K_ij| - 1). Whether the variety fills it is a different
question, and the answer decides what a second-order test even means.
Fano/Heawood: n = 7, q = 7, (a,b) = (3,3)
  linearised tangent dimension 63 (rank 63), conjugation orbit dim 21
    kind  count     eps   max dist  dist/eps  dist/eps^2     verdict
   cross     42   0.080   0.012565    0.1571       1.963     TANGENT
    same     21   0.080   0.113072    1.4134      17.667 not tangent
  MEASURED: at Fano the cross ratios distance/eps^2 are 1.963, 1.991, 1.998 and the
  same-group ratios distance/eps are 1.0016, 1.0004, 1.0001; at AG(2,3) the cross
  ratios are 1.963 and 1.998 and the same-group ratios are 1.4128 and 1.4107. Same
  exponents at both families, different constant.
  A direction is tangent when the nearest point of the variety to A0 + eps D sits at
  distance O(eps^2); at distance O(eps) it is not. If the cross directions are tangent
  and the same-group ones are not, the variety does not fill the linearised space and
  the commuting point is a SINGULAR point of it. The cone is also not a linear
  linearised space, the set of tangent directions is then not closed under
  subtraction: the tangent cone is not a linear subspace, and a Hessian on the
  linearised space is the wrong object. That is why code/curvature.py computes the
  second-order coefficient along explicit curves instead.
  The obstructed configuration needs only two hyperedges sharing w and both missing
  v, which exists in every commuting tight family with a >= 2 and enough vertices, so
  the mechanism is not special to these two. That is an argument about when the
  DIRECTION exists, not a proof that it is always obstructed; two families measured is
  what is actually in hand.
