(e) Is it a WEIGHTED MATCHING POLYNOMIAL? That needs the transversal weight to factor
through pairwise data. The s=3 weight is b^3 - b(t_kl+t_km+t_lm) + 2 tr(A_k A_l A_m), so
the question is whether the triple trace is a function of the three pairwise traces.
A row has POWER only where two triples share pairwise data; on a generic deformed family
every triple has its own class, all groups are singletons, and the test can see nothing.
A first version judged those rows as confirming factorisation, which they cannot do.

  A refuting row is two triples with identical pairwise weights and different transversal
  weights, which is exactly the failure of pairwise factorisation. One such row is enough;
  rows marked consistent do not support factorisation, they merely fail to break it.
  P47 HOLDS in all four parts. The deformed polynomial is the generating function of
  PARTIAL TRANSVERSALS OF THE BLOCK SYSTEM WEIGHTED BY SQUARED VOLUME: choose at most
  one representative from each block's range, weight by the squared volume they span,
  sum with alternating sign. On the commuting locus the weight is the indicator that
  the representatives are distinct, which is the SDR count and the matching number of
  the incidence graph; off it the weight is the continuous measure of how independent
  they are. The first four coefficients are then class-wide constants, because the
  Gram determinant only produces closed walks of length at most three there and those
  are fixed by tightness and idempotency; the four-cycle enters at m_4, which is the
  first genuine joint invariant and the reason the cut is at four.
  It is NOT a weighted matching polynomial, and the obstruction is already visible on
  the commuting locus. In the Fano plane any two lines meet in one point, so every
  triple of blocks has the same pairwise data; but three lines are either concurrent
  or form a triangle, and the triple trace is 1 or 0 accordingly. Two triples with
  identical pairwise weights and different transversal weights is exactly the failure
  of pairwise factorisation, in integers, with no deformation needed. So the weight
  is irreducibly higher than pairwise and no edge-weighted graph carries it.
